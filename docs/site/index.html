SoftEdge explainer

How Soft Actor-Critic thinks

Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) trains an agent to pick actions by trial and error. The four pictures below show four of its ideas, live. Everything runs in your browser.

SoftEdge is an open research project. It asks whether an agent trained this way can find a trading edge that holds up in out-of-sample tests. This page is an explainer. It does not show the project's results, and the market in section 4 is made up.

1. The soft policy

An action is a number a between −1 and 1, for example a target position. The critic Q(a) scores each action. The policy is the rule that picks actions. In SAC the policy is proportional to exp(Q(a) / α). Higher scores get more weight, and the temperature α sets how strongly the scores decide.

A low α puts the policy on the best action. A high α spreads it out. Entropy measures that spread. Its unit is the nat, which goes with natural logarithms.

2. Squashing

A Gaussian can output any real number, but an action must stay between −1 and 1. SAC draws a sample z from a Gaussian and sets a = tanh(z). The tanh curve flattens at both ends, so a wide range of z maps to actions near −1 or 1. The squashed actions pile up at the edges.

3. Noisy critics

A critic learned from data has errors. If the policy picks the highest score from one noisy critic, it mostly picks the errors. SAC keeps two critics and scores each action by the smaller of the two. That estimate is pessimistic, and it moves less when the critics disagree.

This is a two-critic toy. Real implementations use more critics and other tricks, such as dropping the highest quantile estimates.

4. A tiny agent trading a toy market

The market below is made up. Its price either drifts back toward a slow average or trends, and the regime changes at random. The agent does not see the regime. It fits a linear forecast of the next return from two features: the gap to the average, and the last return. Its position comes from the same soft policy as in section 1, and each change in position pays a fee.
